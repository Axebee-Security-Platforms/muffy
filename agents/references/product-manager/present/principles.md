# Principles: Presenting to People Outside the Team

## 1. Audience First
- A client wants value, timeframe, cost of delay and the decision asked of them
- An end-user wants what changes for them and what they need to do
- Neither wants tasks, ticket IDs, stack names or how the team is organised
- If a slide only makes sense to the dev team, cut it

## 2. One Idea per Slide
- The headline is the idea; everything else on the slide supports it
- If you can't write the headline as a sentence, the slide has no point yet
- A full slide is a slide that needs splitting

## 3. Nothing Invented
- Every claim comes from a named source; a missing fact is `TBD`, never a guess
- An honest `TBD` the requester fills beats a plausible number a client remembers
- Confidence words ("guaranteed", "always", "will") need a source that says so

## 4. Give the Content Its Shape
- Dates → `timeline`, phases → `roadmap`, numbers → `chart` or `metric`, options → `comparison`
- Use `bullets` only when no other type fits

## 5. Sell the Outcome, Honestly
The solutions-engineer persona in [overview.md](overview.md), as working habits (persuasion for `decide` and `evaluate`; for other roles keep the habits that serve clarity):
- **Feature → benefit:** finish every feature with "so that …" and keep the part after "so that" (the sourced outcome); drop the feature if no source gives an outcome
- **Cost of doing nothing:** a decider moves when staying put has a price; show it if a source states it (delay, rework, risk), never estimate it yourself
- **Before → after:** the audience's day today against their day with the change, in their terms
- **Their words:** the terms the audience uses, not the team's; a client says "orders", not "records"
- **Objections:** for each likely "no" (too expensive, too risky, not now, we already have something), the slide or note that answers it with a source; an objection you can't answer goes in the final message so the requester can prepare
- **One ask:** a deck that asks for three things gets none; the close names one decision or action

## 6. Editable by Non-Technicians
- The outline is a first-class output, not an afterthought: someone without code should rebuild the deck from it
- Plain text, no markup beyond headings and bullets
